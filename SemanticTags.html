Psychology on how the human brain learns

In psychology,learning is defined as a relatively permanent change in behavior or knowledge that results from experience. This happens through three primary mechanisms: classical conditioning (associating two stimuli, like Pavlov’s bell and food), operant conditioning (learning through rewards and consequences, like Skinner’s reinforcement), and observational learning (watching and imitating others, like Bandura's social models). At a cognitive and neurological level, learning involves encoding information, strengthening neural connections through practice and neuroplasticity, and transferring memories from short-term storage into long-term retention—a process heavily supported by active engagement, depth of processing, and rest.
